# ADR 0022: Use legislation-derived examples to verify recycling calculations

Status: Proposed — independent legal examples and PR comparison, not an assertion of legal advice or an accepted implementation decision.

## Context and decision

Use the numerical cases below as an independent oracle for full-year packaging recycling obligations. Derive expected values from legislation before inspecting implementation; keep implementation observations separate. Inputs are assumed to have already been classified as relevant non-exempt producer packaging, unless a case says otherwise. Results are mathematical tonnes before any output rounding. The legislation specifies rounding input material tonnage to the nearest tonne, but does not in Schedule 5 specify a midpoint rule or rounding the resulting obligation. The Environment Agency (EA) methodology gives operational output-rounding rules; the midpoint rule remains inferred rather than specified by Schedule 5.

Sources: [2024 Regulations, regulation 40](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2024/1332/regulation/40), [Schedule 5](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2024/1332/schedule/5), [regulation 24](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2024/1332/regulation/24), [Schedule 9](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2024/1332/schedule/9), and [regulation 62](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2024/1332/regulation/62). See also [Schedule 15](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2024/1332/schedule/15), the [2025 amendment regulations](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2025/1369/made), the [EA October 2026 methodology](https://npwd.environment-agency.gov.uk/FileDownload.ashx?FileId=bf356d12-30aa-4cb9-abb5-50f7c04870d6) and its [worked examples](https://npwd.environment-agency.gov.uk/FileDownload.ashx?FileId=460d1c8c-98f0-416a-b294-9a20d2a7ad7b). Legislation.gov.uk's revised Schedule 5 was consulted on 5 November 2026. This is a test specification, not a legal opinion; confirm ambiguous rounding, validation, and data-contract requirements with policy owners.

For obligation year Y, use the preceding calendar year's relevant UK supply, rounded to the nearest tonne for each specified category, excluding packaging exempt at supply or in Y. Multiply by the target for Y. For 2025, Schedule 15 can require scaling part-period data and adjusting for transitional packaging before the calculation; the cases below assume those adjustments have already been made. Glass re-melt is a part of the glass obligation: supply × glass target × re-melt target. Regulation 40 requires PRNs or PERNs to demonstrate compliance. A group registration aggregates individual members' obligations (Schedule 9 paragraph 5). Relevant mid-year cases are subject to the detailed conditions of Schedule 9 paragraphs 7–17. Disposal fees are a separate calculation under regulation 62 and are outside the recycling cases below.

## Independent calculation cases

All supplies below are previous-year tonnes and inputs are pre-rounded unless otherwise stated. The target table is an exhaustive year/material matrix: for each cell, test 100 tonnes yields the numeric percentage in tonnes; for glass also test 100 × glass target × re-melt share. Thus glass re-melt outputs for 2025–2030 are 55.5, 57.76, 60.06, 62.4, 64.78 and 68 tonnes respectively.

| ID | Scenario and input | Expected outcome / oracle |
|---|---|---|
| C01 | 2026: 100 plastic, 200 paper, 100 glass | 57 plastic, 154 paper, 76 glass, of which 57.76 re-melt. Glass re-melt is not added to 76. |
| C02 | Each material, zero tonnes | Zero material obligation and zero glass re-melt; no divide-by-zero. |
| C03 | 2026, one tonne of each category | 0.57 plastic, 0.46 wood, 0.62 aluminium, 0.81 steel, 0.77 paper/composite, 0.76 glass; glass re-melt 0.5776 tonnes before any output rounding. |
| C04 | 2025 plastic supply 100; 2024 supply 300; calculate 2026 | 57 tonnes, using 2025 supply and 2026 target. |
| C05 | 2026 plastic eligible supply 100 plus 20 exempt at supply, or 20 exempt in 2026 | 57 tonnes if the 20 is excluded; avoid double exclusion when both exemption conditions apply. |
| C06 | Each category raw weight immediately below and above a half-tonne boundary, e.g. 1.49 and 1.51 tonnes | Nearest-tonne inputs are 1 and 2 respectively before target application. Exactly 1.50 is an unresolved midpoint policy; test only after its rule is established. |
| C07 | 2026 group of two qualifying members, each with 100 tonnes plastic | 57 tonnes per member; aggregate 114 tonnes. Check aggregation does not apply a target a second time. |
| C08 | 2026 group, each member has 1.49 tonnes plastic | Each member's supply rounds to 1 tonne. Unrounded arithmetic is 0.57 each, 1.14 total; applying the EA method of rounding each entity up gives 1 + 1 = 2 tonnes. |
| C09 | 2026 group joiner does not independently meet thresholds; joins 1 July; assume for illustration 184 of 365 days of group membership; full-year plastic obligation 57 tonnes | Under that assumption the unrounded proportion is 57 × 184/365 = 28.734246575… tonnes. Date inclusivity and partial-year rounding order need regulator confirmation; a regulator example uses 60/365 for a 1 November join, where inclusive counting gives 61. |
| C10 | As C09, but independently meets applicable threshold criteria | Full-year obligation under paragraph 9(1)(a), rather than days-proportioned. |
| C11 | A group-registered member leaves during the year; first holding company responsible under paragraph 13 or 16 as applicable | Original holding company remains responsible for the whole-year obligation; do not automatically prorate away the post-leaving portion. |
| C12 | Reusable packaging with agency-satisfied system and at least four-year life | Its obligation may be discharged in four equal annual instalments from first use, subject to the statutory conditions. Distinguish reusable packaging from exempt reused packaging; confirm the relevant years and instalment rounding before calculating an expected result. |
| C13 | 2024 or 2031 target requested | Schedule 5 table supplies no target for that year. Do not invent or silently reuse a target; resolve applicable law/configuration. |
| C14 | Negative, missing, non-finite or unrecognised input codes; missing target | Statute does not define a software error contract. These should not silently become a valid positive obligation; test rejection or explicit upstream validation according to agreed interface. |
| C15 | Large producer threshold boundaries in regulation 24 | Both strict thresholds must be exceeded under regulation 24(1). Packaging is rounded to the nearest tonne first: 50.4 tonnes rounds to 50 and fails, while 50.6 rounds to 51 and passes the weight threshold; £2m exactly fails the turnover threshold. The 50.5 midpoint needs a rule. Threshold packaging excludes packaging exempt at supply; Schedule 5 also excludes packaging exempt in the obligation year. Check relevant financial-year and Y−2 dates, group aggregation and other qualifications separately. |

Boundary inventory: these are selected cases, not exhaustive coverage of the legislation. Other relevant boundaries include scheme changes (regulation 46), insolvency and successors (regulation 27), mergers and transfers (regulations 27A–27C), seller packaging in thresholds (regulation 22), the paper/composite change in regulation 7(5A), and Schedule 15 transitional data. Maximum numeric size, duplicate records, invalid configuration are interface boundaries rather than prescribed numerical rules; date inclusivity requires policy clarification; establish their contracts before assigning a statutory expected value. The table is not a claim that every legal exception or every possible input has been exhausted.

### Consequences and open questions

- The legal oracle remains independent of the implementation and legacy parity corpus. Before promoting the examples into executable acceptance tests, confirm the 2026 application of the EA method, midpoint input rounding, and the treatment of partial-year calculations and instalments. The EA publishes output ceilings; its worked example separately rounds paper and composite before aggregation. Midpoint handling is supported by the workbook and legacy practice but is not explicit in Schedule 5.
- Agree ownership and evidence for prior-year selection, exemptions, thresholds, reusable packaging and group-event responsibility. The current standalone calculator cannot establish them from its inputs.
- The PR's tests include all six years, glass rounding, zero, midpoint and large kilogram values, invalid submissions and configuration. The PR reports passing suites; they were not independently rerun for this document. No conclusion about production end-to-end calculation follows from those tests while the caller and processor are deferred.
- Review scope was limited to calculation behaviour and its test/configuration contracts against these examples; it was not a complete review of all PR files, deployment configuration or the wider legal framework.

### Additional review evidence and unresolved boundaries

- The EA's methodology rounds each entity's obligation up before aggregation and treats glass other as total less re-melt. The mathematical Schedule 5 results in the cases above are pre-rounding; the EA methodology provides a separate operational result. Its October 2026 document applies from 2027; its 2025 method was found on a [third-party mirror](https://www.360environmental.co.uk/wp-content/uploads/2025/04/Calculating-Recycling-Obligations-Direct-Registrant.pdf), and a 2026-specific edition was not located.
- EA guidance treats other material as having no recycling target; `OT` is a valid reporting category, although this calculator rejects it. Agree whether the caller filters it or the calculator accepts it. Packaging type filtering, including `RU` and `OW`, also belongs at a defined boundary. The `GL` output is glass other, not a separate material; `GR` repeats input tonnage and must not be added to `GL` input tonnage.
- The EA workbook's glass example appears to use unrounded input for re-melt, while Schedule 5 paragraph 2(3) says to round L to the nearest tonne. For 2027 and 23,400 kg, the calculator gives 14 tonnes re-melt and 4 glass other; applying the workbook's unrounded approach gives 15 and 3. Seek clarification from the EA rather than presenting the workbook as conclusive.
- For a group joiner, the example day-count convention and the order of rounding and prorating remain unresolved. A leap year has 366 days. The existing whole-tonne output cannot reconstruct exact unrounded totals if a later step requires them. Agree the rule before partial-year work.
- For 2025, Schedule 15 paragraphs 5–6 can require scaling incomplete reported periods and adjusting transitional packaging. For example, applying the 182/61 factor to 1,000 kg gives approximately 2,983.6 kg; the 2025 EA methodology describes rounding to 2,984 kg. The current calculator cannot check whether these adjustments occurred upstream.
- The independent review was limited to the calculator, its tests and the cited legal materials. Regulator positions version 8 and 2026-specific methodology were not available to the reviewer; live RPD behaviour was not verified.

A production `NOLOCK` existence query returned 1 for an active direct-registrant calculation whose producer ID differs from its submitter ID. This is evidence of the stored input shape, subject to dirty-read limitations; it does not establish group membership or a count. Support for different producer IDs within a direct registrant is deferred to MO-701; the calculator currently rejects them.
